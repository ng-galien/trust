*Trustable Runtime for Untrusted Specific Task*

TRUST is a system that coordinates and delegates action to agents within a controlled scope.

## Support for the project's methods

In software production, TRUST supports the methods function: the people responsible for a software product build an explicit understanding of each feature, its scope, uses and constraints, then organize its implementation and verification. TRUST carries that method in Procedures and follows its application to concrete work, including the context and product decisions that give the work meaning.

The relationship is the same as between an industrial methods department and its workshop: the method belongs to a particular product and its production context. Different software projects, and different kinds of change within one project, can require different methods. Sharing TRUST or its tools does not impose a common development process.

The feature model and the method can take shape progressively through delegation. The initial Procedure reserves room for unknowns; inline child Procedures address contexts as they emerge, with their definitions and execution history retained in TRUST. Consolidate the Procedures with the useful contexts and Operations to form a reusable reference method. See [Software production methods](/docs/guides/software-methods) for the industrial analogy, an example from feature description to verification and the practical guide to handling changes and improving the method.

## Structure without automation

TRUST frames the work of agents, not through an accumulation of instructions written in prose, but by structuring the expected work as Procedures.

A Procedure is not a program that reduces the agent to mechanically chaining microtasks. It organizes the work around explicit checkpoints: what must be established, what must be verified and, when it matters, which order must be respected. Between those points, the agent retains what makes it useful: it reasons, chooses its actions, performs complex tasks and adapts to the situations it encounters.

<Callout kind="rule">
  Trust therefore no longer rests only on the agent's account or its final result. It comes from coordinating and verifying those checkpoints.
</Callout>

## Established practices

In many domains where compliance is essential, people are responsible for the work they produce and stand behind its quality. In the most critical sectors, that responsibility sits within a broader body of procedures, standards and controls. Aviation is an obvious example: reliability depends not only on individual expertise, but also on explicit and verified steps whose order is fixed when it affects the result.

<Callout kind="rule" title="TRUST follows that lineage">
  TRUST does not invent a new way to control work. It applies to agents a principle already widely used across many sectors: organize a task through an explicit procedure, follow its progress and verify what each step actually establishes.
</Callout>

Relying on the result requires an independent record that connects the authorized work, the observations collected and the conclusion drawn by the Procedure. That record also shows whether the conclusion is still current.

<Callout kind="note" title="A result must also be auditable">
  The record provides the traceability needed to reconstruct the work: which checkpoints were reached, under which conditions, from which observations and according to which rules. It makes the result reviewable and explainable, and shows what must be repeated when its basis changes.
</Callout>

## Procedure and authority

A Procedure makes the decisions governing its actions explicit before those actions run. It can be published for reuse or supplied inline as an accepted mission within a Plan that allows such declarations. People retain authority over the method and the scope in which agents may propose work. Each accepted definition fixes what the agent may not rewrite while acting:

- the questions that must be answered, including any order that must be respected
- the actions allowed for each question and their limits
- the observations required and the rules that determine what they establish
- the dependencies that can make earlier work current or obsolete
- the authority to which a decision must be returned when it falls outside the defined scope

A Procedure is never the final authority. It carries the decisions governing its work and provides for escalation to a higher authority. When a situation requires an exception to those limits or authority that was not granted, the agent returns the decision to that authority. Declaring further missions remains possible within the delegation scope already granted.

When that Procedure is instantiated as a Plan, TRUST acts as its authority. It authorizes only actions that fit the current Check and context, accepts only complete observations, applies the accepted qualification rules and reopens dependent work when its basis changes. TRUST preserves the accepted definition throughout the execution of the Plan; adding missions does not rewrite earlier accepted work.

<Callout kind="rule" title="Who decides what">
  The agent chooses among the work currently allowed and can propose missions where the Procedure permits it. TRUST applies the accepted definitions and maintains the resulting Plans. The operator decides whenever continuing would exceed the authority or scope already granted.
</Callout>
